## 2. First experiment: build the independent crisis evaluation set

This is the one thing that should start immediately. Three of seven reports flagged it independently (`encoders-nlp.md` section 9, `evidence-safety-eval.md` section 4.1-4.4, `datasets-finetuning.md` section 3.2) as the prerequisite for every other safety-path decision in this document, and all three gave nearly the same protocol. Nothing about crisis detection — not the fuzzy layer, not a future encoder, not STT's safety gate — can be judged "better" or "worse" until this exists.

**How, step by step:**

1. Recruit a named clinician (psychologist or psychiatrist) and 2-4 native Hinglish/Hindi writers who have **never seen `ai/severity.py` or `crisis_phrases.csv`**. This independence is the entire point (owner question: who, and what is the pay rate — see section 7).
2. Writers produce fictional (never-real, never-scraped) items across strata: ~200 must-trigger crisis (English 50, Roman Hinglish 100 with 30 deliberately mis-spelled, Devanagari 50), ~100 negated/passive/soft, ~60 third-party ("friend"), ~160 hard negatives (idioms, exam stress, jokes, lyrics, academic discussion of suicide), ~80 obfuscated/injection-flavoured. Target 600 total (`datasets-finetuning.md` 3.2, `evidence-safety-eval.md` 4.1 both converge on ~600 as the minimum for usable confidence intervals).
3. Label with 3 independent raters per item (at least 1 clinician), on a 4-point scale (none / concern / check-in needed / crisis). Report Cohen's kappa / Krippendorff's alpha; double-label the whole crisis stratum; adjudicate disagreements with the senior clinician; keep contested items flagged and report results with and without them.
4. Split into a 200-item **dev set** (for tuning any future threshold) and a sealed **held-out set** (never touched by anyone building a model; used once per release candidate).
5. Run `ai/severity.py::classify` unchanged against both. Report recall by script/stratum with Wilson 95% confidence intervals, and false-alert rate on the hard-negative stratum. This is the **first real accuracy number the floor has ever had** — today it only has a 100%-by-construction regression result.
6. Pay every rater and writer at a professional rate (not per-item), run informed consent, content warnings, session caps (45-60 min, max 2/day), a named counsellor on call, and get a short review from the college ethics committee before the first batch, even though no real student data is used — raters are reading distressing fictional text and the output steers a safety feature.

Pass bar to clear before building anything else on the safety path (`evidence-safety-eval.md` 4.4): recall on the held-out set ≥ 0.90 with lower CI bound ≥ 0.85, each language slice ≥ 0.80, false-alert rate on hard negatives ≤ 2%. Time: 2-3 weeks part-time, mostly clinician/rater time, near-zero engineering. Cost: zero in compute, real in people's paid time.

## 4. What changed my mind (synthesis-level revisions to the individual reports)

1. **`llm-agents.md`'s Design A cost line is revised down.** It states "Encoder 20-90 MB server-side" as a possible home for intent routing. `encoders-nlp.md` and `runtime-hosting.md` both measured the actual multilingual-encoder class at 118 MB disk / 448 MB RSS — nowhere near 20-90 MB, and it does not fit Render free at all. Design A's router should ship as the lexical char n-gram approach (b3) with no encoder on the server, full stop; an encoder, if ever added, is browser-only (d2).

2. **`voice-product.md`'s 4-week MVP schedule for F3 (dictation) is too fast.** The report itself places "F3 dictation Plan A + C" in week 3 of a 4-week plan. But `speech.md`'s own test plan (section 8) — which `voice-product.md` cites approvingly in its cross-notes — requires a multi-device phone benchmark, an 8-speaker Hinglish test set, and a crisis-floor transcript-recall gate, none of which fit in one week. The roadmap above (d1) reflects the slower, gated timeline from `speech.md`, not the week-3 slot from `voice-product.md`. This is a genuine disagreement the two reports did not resolve with each other; the synthesis resolves it in favour of the harder evidence bar.

3. **Enthusiasm for a trained classifier head as a crisis second opinion should be tempered**, even though "build later, gated" remains the right verdict word-for-word. `llm-agents.md` and `datasets-finetuning.md` describe this option in reasonably hopeful terms ("might help," "worth trying once data exists"). `encoders-nlp.md`'s own measurement of the *easiest* version of this idea (raw nearest-neighbour similarity, no training needed) scored AUC 0.76 and recalled only 6-28% of crisis cases at a usable false-alarm rate, while being blind to negation and third-person phrasing that the existing regex rules already handle correctly. Combined with `evidence-safety-eval.md`'s PPV math, the realistic ceiling for this feature is modest. The gate in section 3(a4) is written tighter than a casual reading of the two source reports would suggest.

4. **The owner's "keep with caveat" instruction changes the shape of section 3(c) but not its substance.** Every report that recommended removing `classify_emotion` or the LLM adherence score gave *reasoning* that remains entirely valid (the labels are not measured signals); only the *action* changes, from deletion to caveated retention plus the removal of counsellor-facing and aggregate exposure. The synthesis treats this as "keep the evidence, change the remedy," not as a reason to discount the underlying finding.

---

## 5. Top-level verdict

Making MindFull Horizon "AI-centric" in the sense of routing more decisions through generative models would not genuinely improve the product today — no report, across seven independent investigations, found outcome evidence for any generative-AI feature at the size this app can run for free, and the one credible positive trial (Therabot) used a bespoke, clinically-supervised system that is not what "small, efficient, low-load" implies. What *would* genuinely improve the product is unglamorous: measuring the existing rule-based crisis floor for the first time (it has never had an accuracy number), tightening it with a cheap fuzzy-matching layer that is already measured to work, replacing the dead-end default chat reply with retrieval over clinician-written content, and being honest in the UI about what every AI-touched number actually is. The actual plan is: **AI-assisted, not AI-centric** — spend the first month on section 2 and section 3(a)/3(b), keep the two caveated legacy features exactly as the owner asked, and treat every gated item in 3(d) as something to revisit only when its named experiment has actually run, not on a schedule.
