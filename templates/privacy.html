Why we use it and our legal basis

We use your data to run the features you use: showing your history, scoring screenings, replying in chat, booking and running appointments, and alerting counsellors when the safety check fires. We do not sell your data and we do not use it for advertising.

The legal basis is your consent under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. You give it when you create your account, and you can withdraw it at any time (see Your rights). Withdrawing consent means we stop using your data and delete your account.

Who can see your data

  • You see everything in your account.
  • Your assigned counsellor sees individual records only for the data your assignment allows. An assignment is created when you book an appointment with that counsellor, or when one is added manually. Counsellor pages do not show journal entries.
  • Doctors and the pharmacy desk. A doctor you have booked can issue and see your prescriptions. A counsellor you have booked can see the medicine names, doses and dates of your prescriptions and the medicines you log, but not the diagnosis or allergies. The pharmacy desk of your institution sees a prescription only when you send it to the desk or give its number. Every such view is written to the audit log.
  • Counsellors at your institution see group totals only, and only when the group is above a minimum size so that no one can be picked out. They also see unacknowledged crisis alerts for their institution, even without an assignment, so a safety alert is never missed.
  • The audit log records every counsellor view of an individual record or crisis alert.
  • The operator and anyone with access to the database or server can technically read stored data. Access is limited to people who run the service.

We may disclose data if a law or a court order requires it, or to prevent serious harm to a person's life.

Cookies and local storage

  • A session cookie keeps you signed in. It is needed for the service to work. It is signed so it cannot be altered, and it lasts up to 24 hours.
  • A consent cookie or setting remembers your choice about optional analytics.
  • A theme preference (light or dark) is stored in your browser.
  • Some pages keep small preferences, such as music filters or an unsaved blog draft, in your browser's local storage. They do not leave your device.
  • First-party analytics run only if you accept them. They record page names and campaign tags, with no user ID and no IP address, and never anything you type. If you decline, nothing is recorded.

We do not use advertising cookies.

Your rights

Under the DPDP Act you can:

  • Access and export your data, and delete your account, from your data page.
  • Correct details such as your name and institution from your profile, or ask us to fix a record through the contact page.
  • Withdraw consent at any time by deleting your account, or by writing to us.
  • Nominate another person to exercise your rights if you cannot.
  • Complain to us first (see Contact and grievances). If we do not resolve it, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Children

Accounts are for people aged 18 or older. You confirm this when you sign up. We do not knowingly keep data of people under 18. If you think a child has an account, tell us through the contact page and we will remove it.

Security

What is in place today:

  • Passwords are stored as hashes, not as plain text.
  • Pages are meant to be served over HTTPS in production, and the session cookie is marked secure and HTTP-only there.
  • Security headers are set on responses, and forms are protected against cross-site request forgery.
  • Login, sign-up and contact forms are rate limited.
  • Counsellor access to individual records is limited by assignment and written to an audit log.

Clinical and personal free text (clinical notes, journal entries, mood notes, screening answers, voice transcripts, your safety plan, appointment notes and rejection reasons, prescription diagnosis/allergies/current medicines, and notification messages) is encrypted at rest in the database with a key the operator holds and can use to decrypt it. We make no claim of certification under HIPAA or similar standards. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your data, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.
